Case Study: Upvoit - The "Secret" Re-Architecture Mission

A complete architectural overhaul of a live SaaS platform in just 15 days.

Excelhunter Technologies
2024
Field Management SaaS
15-Day Sprint
Clean Architecture
Live Platform

What We Cared About (Key Metrics)

We were building a massive Field Management SaaS, and things were moving fast. Success wasn't just about uptime here; it was about keeping the development team from burning out.

Development Velocity

We needed to handle new requirements daily without the code turning into spaghetti.

Team Sanity

Stopping the daily frustrations and conflicts between developers working on the same codebase.

Turnaround Time

We pulled off a complete re-architecture in just 15 days while the app was technically live.

Scalability

Building a backend that could actually handle the load, not just today, but years from now.

The Challenge

We were in the thick of building Upvoit, running live tests to get real customer feedback. The problem was, the feedback loop was too fast. We were getting new requirements every single day.

It became a nightmare to manage. We had features piling up, changes conflicting, and deployment was scary.

The breaking point was the team morale. I was working with Nandlal Kaneriya, and we could "smell" that something was wrong. Bharat Radadiya, who was leading the .NET team, we were discussing this every other day, totally frustrated. Things were breaking, merge conflicts were constant, and we realized: This isn't going to work. We can't build a skyscraper on a shaky foundation.

The Solution

We needed a reset button, but we couldn't just stop the business. So, we came up with a plan—a "secret" plan. We didn't even tell the top management at first because it sounded crazy to restart the architecture of a live product.

I gathered the core squad: myself, Nandlal Kaneriya, Bharat Radadiya, and we pulled in Henil Patel and consulted with our senior dev, Kishan Prajapati. We locked ourselves in a room (metaphorically and literally) to figure this out.

We decided to re-architect the whole thing from scratch, focusing on how multiple developers could work together without stepping on each other's toes.

Clean Architecture

We moved away from the messy code to a strict Clean Architecture. This kept our business logic safe and separate from everything else.

CQRS (Command Query Responsibility Segregation)

We separated the "reads" from the "writes." It sounds complex, but it meant our reporting could be fast without slowing down the data entry.

Multiple Databases

Instead of stuffing everything into one giant DB, we split things up where it made sense.

.NET Best Practices

We went deep into .NET patterns—Unit of Work, Repository patterns—to make sure the code was modular and testable.

The Business Impact

It was a gamble, but it paid off. We worked double-time, balancing regular feature requests with this secret overhaul.

The 15-Day Sprint

Somehow, through late nights and pure focus, we managed to push the new architecture live in just 15 days.

Real Talk

Let's be real—Bharat Radadiya still comes to me with concerns, and we still have problems. Nothing is ever perfect. But the difference is that now, the issues are trackable. We can identify bugs and fix them quickly instead of being lost in chaos. The conversations shifted from pure frustration to "Okay, here's the fix."

Shouldering the Growth

Upvoit now runs on a backend that is solid enough to handle the traffic, new features, and upcoming growth on its shoulders.

A Strong Start

We know continuous improvements are always needed, but you have to start somewhere. This re-architecture gave us the solid ground we needed to stop fighting fires and start building the future.
