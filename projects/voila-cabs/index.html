Case Study: The Voila Cabs Story - Scaling Our Tech for Growth

Taking our backend from a startup setup to a high-availability platform ready for serious growth.

What We Cared About (Key Metrics)

When you're running a cab platform, the tech has to work. For this project, we knew 'success' wasn't just about code, it was about real-world results. We boiled it down to a few key things:

Platform Uptime

We wanted to get as close to 100% uptime as possible. No more crashes during peak hours.

App Speed

Making sure the app felt fast and responsive for users, even when it was super busy.

Peace of Mind (Our Data)

Knowing our data was safe, backed up, and we wouldn't lose bookings if a server went down.

Room to Grow

Being able to handle 5x or 10x more users without the system breaking a sweat.

Less Risk

Simply put, we wanted to sleep at night, not worrying about a server failing and taking the business offline.

The Challenge

We had a good problem: Voila Cabs was getting popular, fast.

But our tech was starting to creak under the pressure. The platform—which included our mobile apps, the website, and the admin panel for tracking drivers—was running on a backend that just wasn't built for that kind of traffic.

The main issue was our databases. We were running single instances of MySQL and MongoDB. This was fine when we started, but it was becoming a huge risk. We were staring down a few big problems:

Spotty Reliability

The app would get sluggish or even fail during the busiest times (like a Friday night!).

Major Data Risk

If that one server died, we could lose everything—bookings, driver info, user accounts. It was a classic "single point of failure."

Downtime Nightmares

If we needed to do maintenance or an update, the whole platform had to go offline.

Hitting a Wall

We couldn't just 'add more users.' We were stuck and couldn't scale.

The Solution

I was actually working on the iOS app at first, but it became crystal clear that the real fire was in our backend. A great app doesn't matter if the servers behind it are down.

So, we made the call to pivot and focus on re-architecting our core infrastructure on AWS. Here's what we did, in simple terms:

No More Single Points of Failure

Instead of one database server for each, we set up a 3-replica cluster for both MongoDB and MySQL. Think of it like having a main server and two "hot" backups ready to take over instantly.

Automatic Failover

With this new setup, if the main database (the 'primary') failed, one of the 'secondary' replicas would automatically get promoted to be the new primary. The app would keep running, and users would never even know anything happened.

Spreading the Load (Sharding)

For MongoDB especially, we used sharding. This is a fancy way of saying we split our data across multiple servers. Instead of one server trying to handle all the requests, the load was distributed, making everything much, much faster.

Building on AWS

We did all of this on Amazon Web Services (AWS), which gave us the powerful tools we needed to build and manage this new, resilient setup.

The Business Impact

So, what did all this tech work actually mean for the business? It was a game-changer.

The App Just Worked

Uptime went way up. The platform became the reliable service we always wanted it to be, and we crushed the downtime issues that were hurting our reputation.

Our Data Was Safe

We stopped worrying about data loss. The new system was redundant and backed up, protecting our most valuable assets.

Happier Customers

The app felt faster and more stable. This built trust. When a user needed a cab, they knew Voila would work.

Ready for the Future

We finally had a strong foundation. We could confidently run marketing campaigns and push for more growth, knowing the tech could easily handle it.
